2026 – now

nearbites

Co-founder · NUS Orbital 2026, Apollo

In development · Apollo · tested with 15 students

Food gets shared across campus all the time, but finding it before it goes to waste is mostly luck. I spent the summer learning React Native and co-building nearbites, an app that puts every listing on a map with the collection details attached.

React NativeExpoAppwriteJestGitHub Actions

Why I started

nearbites grew from a real frustration in my own campus life. Food-sharing messages were excessive, easy to miss and often lacked clear collection details, so the food went to whoever happened to be scrolling. Summer gave me the time to learn the tools and build something better.

My contribution

We are a team of two, and I built authentication, onboarding, posting, the feed and the campus map, along with the Appwrite data layer underneath them. Students can filter by distance and expiry, and every listing carries its dietary and pickup details.

What testing changed

Fifteen NUS students tested the app on their own, with no guidance from me. All of them completed onboarding and found listings, and twelve said they would use it. Their feedback pushed us to write clearer pickup instructions and to remove stale posts automatically.

Where it stands

We attained Orbital Apollo, and the core flows are built and tested, though push notifications are still unresolved. The question I am sitting with now is how to turn a product that works into one people come back to.

Engineering

A shared authentication service and React Context keep user state consistent across screens, and Jest tests run through GitHub Actions on every push, covering distance, expiry, visibility and post validation.

Lessons from debugging

Inconsistent date parsing and incorrect upload file sizes were the easy fixes. Testing surfaced a harder one that was a product problem rather than a bug: a correct GPS address still does not tell someone which room to collect the food from.

An unresolved feature

The notification backend works in isolation, but device token registration fails on the device, so end-to-end push delivery is still not working.

2026

ML Research Agent

TikTok CodeJam 2026, Track 2 (Autonomous ML) · five-person team

Completed · validation 0.6016 → 0.6035 · single seed

Our team built an agent that runs its own experiments on a recommender pipeline. I owned the LLM interface, prompts and response handling, which is the layer where a model's suggestion has to become code that actually runs.

PythonNumPyGemini API

The challenge

Over a weekend, our five-person team built an agent that proposes, codes, trains and evaluates its own changes to a recommender pipeline, then decides whether to keep each experiment or revert it.

My contribution

I owned llm.py and prompts.py, which covered prompt construction, code extraction, experiment history, retries and token tracking. The interface takes any compatible provider through configuration rather than hard-coded calls.

The result

Validation improved from 0.6016 to 0.6035 on a single-seed run using CPU and a free API tier. The gain is real but preliminary, and it would need repeated runs across seeds before anyone should trust it.

What I learned

Several different feature proposals produced identical scores, which turned out to be because the encoder sat outside the files the agent was allowed to edit. An agent can only improve what it can reach.

Improving the loop

I added an out-of-fold encoding requirement to close a leakage path, and traced two failure modes that were quietly corrupting generated files: truncated model outputs and Windows encoding mismatches.

Limits

Subprocesses and timeouts keep execution under control, but they do not securely isolate generated code, and the measured improvement still needs validation across seeds.

2026

Beijing housing market analysis

BT2102 · six-person team · R and Tableau

Completed · academic project

Working in a six-person team, we used R and Tableau to turn Beijing transaction data into a view on where to invest, what to develop and when to enter the market.

RTableaut-testForecastingData cleaning

The brief

Given a property-market brief and a raw transaction dataset, our team had to produce investment and development recommendations for specific Beijing districts rather than a general description of the market.

The approach

We cleaned the data, compared districts and property features, then used Welch t-tests, forecasts and an opportunity index so that each recommendation rested on something testable.

The recommendation

We pointed to Chaoyang for premium investment and Xicheng for redevelopment potential. The two differ because the objectives behind them differ, and we said so rather than forcing one answer.

The lesson

Some central districts showed a negative elevator premium, which looked wrong until older housing stock offered an explanation. It taught me to interrogate a pattern before recommending action on it, since an observational comparison never establishes causation on its own.

Data decisions

Days-on-market was missing for roughly half the dataset, so we dropped the variable rather than the rows and kept our findings limited to the urban districts actually represented.

2021 – 2023

Cross-border sourcing

Sole operator

Completed · self-funded throughout

Noticed a streetwear price gap between Japan and Singapore and built a sourcing business on my own money. I handled supplier relationships, pricing and inventory, and later extended the same model into Thailand.

SourcingPricingNegotiationInventory

The opportunity

Frequent trips to Japan made the price gap on identical streetwear pieces impossible to ignore. Conversational Japanese was what turned that into a business, because it let me build real relationships with shop staff and hear about releases early.

What I owned

Everything: sourcing, authentication, pricing and cash flow. With my own savings sitting in inventory, a bad buying decision cost me directly, which is a very effective teacher.

The mistake

I priced early stock from what I thought pieces were worth, and it sat unsold for months. Now I check what things actually transact at before committing a cent.

What stayed with me

Running it taught me to act on an opportunity and own the outcome either way. It also made me restless: reselling someone else's product stopped being enough, and I wanted to build something of my own.

Brand strategyProduct conceptRetailPitching

The idea

We proposed the balance, a gender-equality campaign built around the 550 silhouette and an N X B identity that let the two halves of the logo stand for two halves of an audience.

The result
